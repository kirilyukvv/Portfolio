A library that fills itself

Tinkoff's marketing teams reused creatives by cloning old Jira tasks and digging through a Figma page of templates. Content storage in Touch fills itself when a creative is published, so any creative, illustration, icon or template can be found and traced to its campaigns. Designer Alex led the concept; I reviewed the design, advised on the business process and drew part of the prototypes.

◆ Context
Touch, Tinkoff's internal platform for customer marketing. Storage is where finished creatives and their parts live after production
◆ Research
Part of the February role sessions with 19 specialists (5 editors, 7 designers, 7 technologists), then a May review with platform product and architecture leads
◆ My role
Product Design Lead. Design review, business process expertise, part of the prototypes
◆ Status
Live: creative registry with campaign usage, illustration and icon libraries, template catalog. Not yet: performance per creative and revision history. The template model is still open

Overview

Campaign creation gave the campaign a home in Touch. Content production is where editors, designers and technologists make the creatives inside it. This case is about what happens after: where finished creatives and the pieces they are made of live, and how the next team finds them.

Before storage, the history of a creative was spread across a Jira task, a Figma file and the memory of whoever made it. Reuse worked only if you knew whom to ask.

Problem

  • Reuse meant cloning. "We take a finished task, clone it and tweak it a bit." That is how the platform architect described the usual way to reuse content.
  • Templates lived outside the product. Editors kept a whole Figma page of story templates, and Alex kept it open every day. A team that needed a new layout waited for whoever maintained the constructor.
  • Building blocks had no names. Ready-made story elements in the constructor had random names, so technologists found them from memory.
  • A second library was about to appear. The design team already ran a library of validated, tagged illustrations and planned to serve it company-wide. A separate library in Touch would split the source of truth.
  • Overuse was invisible. Designers asked for a way to see how often a creative had been reused, so the same visual doesn't end up in every campaign.

Approach

Storage was not a separate track. It was part of every February session with editors, designers and technologists: after walking through their own mode, each role saw the library and told us what they search for and where. Editors and designers had the most to say.

  • Editors wanted to choose between story layouts with lists, facts and ratings instead of one standard template.
  • Designers asked us to plug in their validated library instead of building a second one, and to show how often a creative is reused.

In May we brought the concept to the platform's product and architecture leads. Their objections are described below.

Key decisions

Decision 01 · Live

The library fills itself

Why
A library that depends on people uploading and tagging goes stale. Every creative in Touch already carries what tagging needs: the campaign, the scenario, the Jira task, the test group.
How
When a creative is published to a campaign, it lands in storage together with its images and icons, tagged with that metadata. Manual upload stays for assets made outside Touch.
Instead of
A separate asset manager that someone has to fill and keep in order by hand.

Decision 02 · Live, metrics next

Every creative answers "where is this used?"

Why
A creative can run in several campaigns and touchpoints at once. Before changing or reusing it, people need to see all of them.
How
A creative is a record, not a picture. Its panel shows display conditions, including the dark theme, the campaigns that use it and the touchpoints where it appears, with a link back to the builder.
Instead of
A gallery of previews, where finding usage means asking around.
Status
Usage is live. Performance per creative is not: the architecture team wants analytics to live outside the content system (see below).

Decision 03 · Changed after research

One library with the design team, not two

Why
The design team already curates validated, tagged illustrations and plans to serve them company-wide. A second library in Touch would compete with theirs.
Before
The concept had Touch build its library only from its own creatives.
After
One-way sync. Validated images flow from the design team's library into Touch, and nothing flows back. Touch adds what comes from its own published creatives.

Decision 04 · Open

Template or branch?

Why
Editors and technologists need starting points, so they don't rebuild common layouts or wait for the constructor team.
Live
A catalog of banner templates that shows each layout's structure: text, icon, buttons, badge, image, progress bar.
Alternative
The platform architect argued that templates don't need to be a separate object. Any creative can be marked as a favourite and branched, and the history of who changed what comes for free.
Open
The two answer different needs: a catalog gives structure, branching reuses proven content. Which model holds the data is not decided yet.

Where the concept was challenged

In May the platform architect pushed back on three points. Content needs a master system with versions and revisions, where anyone can see who changed what and what it was branched from. Templates don't need to be a separate entity. And analytics doesn't belong in the content system at all, apart from unique IDs for each piece of content and its variants.

Versions and revisions are not in storage yet. On analytics, the first version shows usage, not performance. On templates, the catalog shipped because teams needed it, but the data model behind it is still open. Instead of settling this in the room, I proposed listing every disputed point and checking each one with users.

Outcome

  • Live: creative registry with campaign usage and touchpoints, illustration and icon libraries that fill themselves from published creatives, template catalog.
  • Changed after research: one-way sync with the design team's library instead of a second library.
  • Open: template entity or branching, revision history, performance per creative, a reuse counter that designers asked for.

Learnings

  • A library stays alive only if filling it costs nothing. Metadata from the campaign does the tagging that people never get around to.
  • Don't build a second library next to a working one. The design team already owned validated illustrations. Syncing with them was cheaper than competing with them.
  • Agree on what a word means before arguing about it. To an editor a template is a layout; to an architect it can be a branch of old content. Naming both turns the dispute into a concrete question.
